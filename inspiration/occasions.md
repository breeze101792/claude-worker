# Occasions & Situations

A reference list of real-world situations where English is needed. Use this to inspire new sentence patterns.

---

## At Work — Day to Day

- Joining a discussion mid-way
- Stating your position on a decision
- Pushing back on a timeline or requirement
- Asking for clarification in a meeting
- Volunteering for a task
- Saying no to extra work without sounding difficult
- Reporting a blocker or delay
- Asking for help when stuck
- Offering help to a colleague
- Confirming next steps after a meeting
- Sharing an update in stand-up
- Giving a quick status in Slack
- Asking for a code review
- Leaving comments on a PR
- Pinging someone you don't know well
- Looping someone into a thread
- Following up on a silent email
- Setting or negotiating a deadline
- Handing off work before vacation (OOO)
- Announcing something to the team

## Presentations & Demos

- Opening a presentation
- Introducing yourself and the topic
- Explaining the agenda and timing
- Moving from one slide to the next
- Zooming in on a detail
- Skipping a section when short on time
- Handling a tough question
- Buying time when you don't know the answer
- Checking if the audience is following
- Asking the audience a question
- Wrapping up and landing the takeaway
- Ending and inviting questions
- Doing a live demo — and something breaks
- Presenting to executives vs. peers
- Presenting remotely (screen sharing, lag)

## Meetings — Specific Moments

- Starting a meeting on time
- Keeping the meeting on track (time boxing)
- Interrupting politely
- Bringing the conversation back to the topic
- Making sure a quiet person gets to speak
- Parking a topic for later
- Confirming a decision before moving on
- Ending the meeting with clear action items
- The awkward first 2 minutes while people join

## Leadership & Management

- Delegating a task clearly
- Giving feedback that's direct but not harsh
- Receiving feedback without getting defensive
- Praising someone's work (specific and genuine)
- Addressing a performance issue
- Motivating the team before a big push
- Announcing a change or re-org
- Taking responsibility when something went wrong
- Defending your team from outside pressure
- Setting a vision or direction
- Running a 1:1
- Saying no to a request from above

## Written Communication

- Writing a short, clear email
- Writing a subject line that gets opened
- Sending a calendar invite with context
- Writing a Slack message that doesn't sound rude
- Documenting a decision for future reference
- Writing meeting notes that others can act on
- Drafting an incident report or postmortem
- Writing a proposal or design doc intro
- Asking a question on a public channel
- Replying-all (and knowing when not to)

## Small Talk & Social at Work

- Monday morning ("How was your weekend?")
- Friday afternoon ("Any plans this weekend?")
- Talking about the weather
- Commuting complaints and stories
- Lunch — suggesting a place, ordering together
- Coffee break chat
- Celebrating a teammate's work anniversary or birthday
- When someone is leaving the team
- Welcoming a new team member
- Office / remote work small talk
- The elevator or hallway 30-second chat

## Social — Outside Work

- Meeting a friend of a friend
- At a party — introducing yourself
- Making plans over text
- Canceling or rescheduling plans
- Catching up with someone you haven't seen in a while
- Talking about movies, shows, music
- Discussing food, restaurants, cooking
- Talking about hobbies and free time
- Telling a short story or anecdote
- Reacting to someone's story (showing interest)

## Traveling

- Checking in at the airport
- Going through security
- At the gate — asking about delays, boarding
- On the plane — talking to seatmates, asking flight attendants
- Going through customs and immigration
- Checking into a hotel
- Asking for a late checkout
- Asking the hotel for extras (towels, toiletries, etc.)
- Asking for directions on the street
- Asking for directions in a station or airport
- Buying a ticket (train, bus, metro)
- Renting a car
- Filling up gas
- Dealing with a canceled or delayed flight
- Lost luggage at baggage claim

## Eating Out

- Making a reservation
- Arriving and checking in at a restaurant
- Ordering food (casual diner vs. nice restaurant)
- Asking about the menu — dietary restrictions, recommendations
- Ordering drinks at a bar
- Telling the server something is wrong with the order
- Asking for the check
- Splitting the bill
- Tipping and paying
- Takeout — ordering on the phone
- Fast food and coffee shop ordering
- Food truck ordering

## Shopping & Transactions

- Browsing in a store ("Just looking, thanks.")
- Asking for a different size or color
- Asking about a return policy
- Returning an item
- Buying electronics — asking for advice
- Grocery store — asking where something is
- Checking out, bagging, paying
- Dealing with a wrong charge or receipt issue
- Online order pickup
- Farmers market — chatting with vendors

## Services & Errands

- At the bank — opening an account, depositing
- Post office — mailing a package
- Pharmacy — picking up a prescription
- Haircut / barber — describing what you want
- Dry cleaning — dropping off and picking up
- Car repair / mechanic — describing the issue
- Oil change and basic car maintenance
- Home repair — calling a plumber, electrician, landlord
- Package delivery issues (missed delivery, wrong address)
- Gym — signing up, asking about equipment, classes

## Health & Medical

- Making a doctor's appointment
- Checking in at the front desk
- Describing symptoms to a doctor or nurse
- Understanding the doctor's instructions
- At the pharmacy — asking about medication
- At the dentist
- At the eye doctor
- Calling a nurse line or advice line
- Urgent care — explaining the situation
- Emergency room
- Calling 911
- Health insurance — calling about a bill or coverage

## Dealing with Problems

- A bill looks wrong — calling customer service
- Internet or cable is down — calling support
- Something you ordered arrived damaged
- A landlord / tenant issue
- Car trouble — calling a tow truck or roadside assistance
- Lost wallet or phone — what to say and who to call
- Credit card fraud — calling the bank
- Noise complaint with a neighbor
- Something broke in the house (appliance, plumbing)

## Phone & Video Calls

- Starting a call — introducing yourself
- Explaining why you're calling
- Audio issues — "You're breaking up."
- Video call — "You're on mute." / "Can you see my screen?"
- Ending a call smoothly
- Leaving a voicemail
- Calling a business (not a person)
- Calling someone you don't know

- Chatting with your dog walker or babysitter
- Tipping etiquette — when and how much
- Making an appointment (dentist, car, hair)
- Talking to a police officer (traffic stop)
- Jury duty — explaining or asking to be excused
- DMV — dealing with the driver's license office
- Renting an apartment — touring, asking questions
- Dealing with a landlord
- Setting up utilities (internet, electric, water)
- Moving — hiring movers, renting a truck

## Education & Learning

- Asking a question in class or workshop
- Explaining what you do for work
- Talking about your background or career path
- Giving a toast at a dinner or event
- Accepting a compliment
- Giving a compliment naturally
